# Activity 10: Academic Referencing (APA & IEEE Formats)

**Objective:** Understand and apply proper academic referencing using either APA or IEEE formats for your course assignments and reports.

Maintaining academic integrity is critical. Whenever you use ideas, data, code, or images that are not your own, you must credit the original author. 

Depending on your specific assignment guidelines, you will typically be asked to use either **APA (American Psychological Association)** or **IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers)** formatting.

---

## 1. APA Format (7th Edition)

APA is an "author-date" style commonly used in the social sciences, business, and many computing disciplines.

### In-Text Citations
When you refer to a source within your text, include the author's last name and the year of publication.
- **Narrative citation:** Smith (2023) argued that AI models...
- **Parenthetical citation:** Recent studies show a decrease in error rates (Smith & Jones, 2023).

### Reference List Examples
At the end of your document, list all sources in alphabetical order by the author's last name.

- **Journal Article:**
  Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. *Title of Periodical, volume number*(issue number), pages.
  > Smith, J., & Doe, E. (2023). The impact of deep learning on data analysis. *Journal of Artificial Intelligence, 15*(2), 123-145.

- **Website:**
  Author, A. A. (Year, Month Date). *Title of page*. Site name. URL
  > OpenAI. (2023, March 14). *GPT-4 Technical Report*. OpenAI Blog. https://openai.com/research/gpt-4

## 2. IEEE Format

IEEE is a "numeric" style predominantly used in engineering, computer science, and information technology.

### In-Text Citations
Sources are cited using a number enclosed in square brackets. Numbers are assigned sequentially based on the order the source first appears in the text.
- **Example:** As demonstrated in recent studies [1], deep learning... Another approach was proposed by Jones [2].

### Reference List Examples
At the end of your document, list sources numerically in the order they were cited in the text (not alphabetically).

- **Journal Article:**
  [#] A. A. Author of article, "Title of article," *Title of Journal*, vol. #, no. #, pp. page number(s), Month year.
  > [1] J. Smith and E. Doe, "The impact of deep learning on data analysis," *J. Artif. Intell.*, vol. 15, no. 2, pp. 123-145, Mar. 2023.

- **Website/Online Document:**
  [#] A. A. Author. (Year, Month). *Title of web page*. [Online]. Available: URL.
  > [2] OpenAI. (2023, Mar.). *GPT-4 Technical Report*. [Online]. Available: https://openai.com/research/gpt-4

## Recommended Tools for Managing References

You do not need to memorize these formats or type them out manually every time. We highly recommend using reference management software:

1. **Zotero (Free):** An excellent, open-source tool that integrates into your browser and word processors (Word, Google Docs) to automatically generate citations and bibliographies.
2. **Mendeley (Free):** Another popular reference manager that includes PDF highlighting and annotation features.
3. **Built-in Tools:** Both Microsoft Word and Google Docs have built-in citation tools that support APA (and sometimes IEEE) formatting.
4. **Google Scholar / Database Export:** Most academic databases (like Google Scholar or the IEEE Xplore library) have a "Cite" button that provides the exact text to copy-paste in your chosen format.
